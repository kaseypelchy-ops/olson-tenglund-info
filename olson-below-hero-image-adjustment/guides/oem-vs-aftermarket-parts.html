OEM vs. aftermarket parts.

A straightforward explanation of replacement part types and what matters most when comparing options.

Home/Parts Help/OEM vs Aftermarket Auto Parts Explained

What is the difference between OEM and aftermarket auto parts?

OEM generally means original equipment manufacturer. In everyday parts shopping, an OEM part is supplied through the vehicle manufacturer's original-equipment channel. An aftermarket part is sold outside that channel by another supplier. Aftermarket parts can range from economy replacements to premium components.

Does aftermarket mean lower quality?

No. “Aftermarket” describes how a part is supplied, not a single quality level. Materials, design, testing, warranty, and manufacturer reputation vary by product. An aftermarket part may meet relevant specifications, but compatibility should be checked for the specific vehicle and application.

What should you compare?

  1. Exact vehicle fitment

    Check year, make, model, engine, drivetrain, trim and production-date restrictions where relevant. Use VIN or original part numbers when needed.

  2. Relevant specifications

    Compare dimensions, materials, electronic connectors and any applicable safety or performance requirements. A visual resemblance is not enough to verify fitment.

  3. Product origin and warranty

    Identify the actual manufacturer and ask about the applicable written warranty. Terms can differ between brands and parts.

  4. Price and availability

    Price is one factor, but correct fit, durability expectations and the work involved in replacement also matter. Contact the store for current options.

Are there parts where extra care is needed?

Yes. Safety-critical braking and steering components, restraint systems and electronically controlled systems require particular attention to specifications and proper installation. Always follow the vehicle manufacturer's requirements and use a qualified technician when appropriate.

Practical takeaway: Ask “Does this specific part meet the requirements for my vehicle?” rather than judging fit solely by an OEM or aftermarket label.
